## (i) Literature

Nothing found on gluing an aperiodic prototile's edges into a surface, nor on
developments of such surfaces. What exists:

1. **S. Langerman, A. Winslow, "A Complete Classification of Tile-makers",
   JCDCGG 2015.** Lemma 3.1: a closed surface is a tile-maker iff every point
   has curvature κ ≥ 0 with (360° − κ) | 360°. Theorem 4.1: besides Akiyama's
   five classes the only closed tile-makers are flat tori, flat Klein bottles
   and projective planes flat except at two points. Theorem 4.2: developments
   of closed tile-makers are exactly the isohedral prototiles; **corollary: no
   anisohedral tile (a fortiori no aperiodic tile) is a development of a
   closed tile-maker.** This closes the door on any closed-surface version of
   the idea: whatever surface we glue from the hat, not all of its
   developments can tile, and those that do are not characterised by the
   surface alone.
2. J. Akiyama, K. Matsunaga, *Treks into Intuitive Geometry* 2nd ed., Ch. 2
   §8–10 "Topologically extended / Flat closed surface tile-maker theorem":
   the book-level account of the same closed-surface results (flat tori and
   Klein bottles); it stays within isohedral tilings.
3. SMKGS, "A chiral aperiodic monotile" (arXiv:2305.17743), Lemma 2.1: the
   Spectre's edges may be replaced by one curve placed alternately (or by an
   s-curve); any curve works if the result is a topological disk.
4. C. S. Kaplan, "The Path to Aperiodic Monotiles" (arXiv:2509.12216): no
   mention of developments, tile-makers or surfaces; edge modification only
   as "altering the outlines of shapes to enforce neighbour relationships".
5. Searches for "Penrose unfolding/development", "Conway criterion aperiodic",
   "orbifold aperiodic monotile", "half-translation surface tiling" returned
   nothing relevant (closest: R. Lang's "Paper Pentasia", an aperiodic
   polyhedral surface built on Penrose kites and darts, which is unrelated).

### Class table (the deliverable)

Klein group V = {I, R, M, RM}; direct contact (i,j): c_j = R c_i (half-turn
about the edge midpoint); mirrored contact: c_j = M c_i (reflection in the
edge line). H = subgroup generated by cycle products.

| tile | class | edges (label relative to the representative) | H | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| hat / turtle / Tile(a,b), all tilings | long edges | 0:I, 5:R, 6:M, 9:R, 10:M, 13:RM | {I, M} | **straight** |
| hat / turtle / Tile(a,b), all tilings | short edges | 1:I, 2:R, 3:I, 4:R, 7:I, 8:R, 11:M, 12:R | {I, M} | **straight** |
| Spectre, chiral hull | all 14 edges | 1:I, 0:R, 2:R, 3:I, 4:R, 5:I, 6:R, 7:I, 8:R, 9:I, 10:R, 11:I, 12:R, 13:I | {I} | **free** (one curve, placed alternately) |

Minimal obstructions for the hat: (0,5,D), (5,6,D), (0,6,M) give
M c_0 = R·R c_0 = c_0; and (1,2,D), (2,11,D), (1,11,M) give M c_1 = c_1. So a
bump on a long (or short) edge would have to be simultaneously a bump and a
notch at the same place.

**Consequence (new as far as the search shows).** Every hat tiling contains
every one of the 31 contact types (the hull is minimal: all tilings are
generated by the primitive substitution), so a generic edge deformation of the
hat that is incompatible with even one contact type admits no tiling at all,
and compatibility with all 31 forces every edge straight. Hence *the hat,
turtle and every Tile(a,b) with reflections admit no non-trivial edge
curving*, whereas the Spectre (chiral hull) admits exactly one free curve
placed alternately — precisely the construction of SMKGS Lemma 2.1, which this
computation recovers independently. "Generic" means the curves have no
accidental congruent sub-arcs beyond those forced by H (DESIGN.md §1.4).

## (iii) The glued surface

Γ is **not a perfect matching** for either tile: every hat edge meets 3–6
different edges in the tilings (e.g. edge 0 meets 5 D, 6 M, 9 D, 10 M; edge
12 meets 1 D, 2 M, 3 D, 4 M, 7 D, 8 M), and every Spectre edge meets 3–5
others. Gluing "the hat's edges in pairs according to Γ" therefore does not
define a surface; there is no canonical pairing to choose (any choice throws
away contact types that occur in every tiling). Gluing whole *classes* gives a
2-complex with one face and one (hat: two) edge(s), not a surface. The
computation of cone angles, Euler characteristic and orientability in
`contacts.py` (`surface()`) is therefore never reached; it is kept for custom
tiles whose Γ is a matching (lattice tiles: there it reproduces the flat torus
/ pillowcase of Akiyama–Langerman–Winslow).

Independently of this, Langerman–Winslow's corollary already rules out an
aperiodic closed tile-maker, so even a hypothetical surface could only have
*some* tiling developments, which is exactly Theorem B restated.

## (iv) Verdict

* **Novelty of the cut-class idea: none.** The surface formulation adds
  nothing beyond the seam principle (DESIGN.md Theorem B); for the hat and the
  Spectre it is not even well defined, and the closed-surface route is closed
  by Langerman–Winslow 2015. Do not make it a theorem.
* **Enlarging the cut class** (cuts through the interior, envelope-style) is
  the literal unfolding, which fails for the hat (DESIGN.md §1.3); cuts not
  isotopic to the edge system would have to be compatible with a different
  contact structure that no hat tiling has.
* **What is worth stating in the paper (as a proposition with the computed
  table as proof, computer-assisted like SMKGS):** the hat family is *rigid*
  under edge curving while the Spectre has a one-curve family; the Klein-group
  calculus explains both in one line each. This is modest but concrete,
  apparently unpublished, and it directly shapes the editor: in Seam mode the
  hat/turtle show "all edges forced straight", the Spectre shows one editable
  class, and lattice tiles show free classes.
